# Backlog

Deferred work, each with a size and the trigger that makes it worth doing. Finishing an item removes its line. Known limits that are deliberate are listed at the end so they are not rediscovered as bugs.

## Studies

- **HMM variants still untried.** Study 32 ran time-of-day and volatility transitions (bucketed), explicit durations (state expansion), window-feature emissions and the daily-plus-minute state, all null against the pre-registered test once the forest is taken as the reference. Left: an HMM on order-flow imbalance alone, a logistic transition model in place of buckets, a state count other than five, entry minutes other than 0 and 3. Size: half a day. Trigger: only if a result elsewhere points at a state variable the forest lacks; the line is otherwise closed (owner, 2026-10-01: tabled, not dropped; studies 29, 30 and 32 say the states are volatility states and add nothing).
- **Which pair of minute-0 groups carries the seconds-entry gain.** The study 31 group table (10 and 30 seconds, the forest on the lead plus one group at a time) found that no single group of the minute-0 set (reversal and lag returns, volatility, one-minute flow, position and clock) reproduces the gain, and that the indicator bank, which was never among study 31's inputs, matches the full set at 10 seconds but not at 30. Open: which pairs of the four groups reproduce it, and whether the indicator bank plus one group does at 30 seconds. Ablation: the forest on the lead plus each of the 6 pairs, then the indicator bank plus each group, same windows and intervals. Size: about 30 fits, 40 minutes (the group run took 21 minutes for 12 fits). Trigger: before a seconds-after-open entry is added to the forward log.
- **Score the derivatives events of the library.** The tick events are scored (docs/horizons.md, "Derivatives and tick events"); the funding, open-interest and liquidation-signature events are defined and tested in `library_events.py` (`deriv_events`) but not run, because the futures files held here cover 2025-10 to 2026-09 and every cutoff is a percentile of the previous year. Needs the futures history for as many years as Binance publishes (which years exist for funding, perp klines and the open-interest metrics is not yet checked) and then a run through the same machinery over those years. Size: the wiring plus the run, about half a day. Trigger: the download finishing. The owner approved the download on 2026-10-05 and `scripts/fetch_futures_history.py --start 2019-09 --end 2025-08` is running (log in `logs/futures_history.log`; months the archive lacks are counted as missing); the coverage table in the doc is regenerated by the run, and the first scorable year is the first full year after each series begins.
- **Does the trade-count climax add anything to the price extreme?** The climax at a new 1-hour high or low reverses as strongly as the library's probes, but the library's wick, pin-bar and volume-climax events select many of the same minutes. Compare a new 1-hour extreme with and without the climax (and the relative count against the day's own average, which the frozen definition does not use), matched on year and hour. Size: two hours, reusing `library_events.py` and the mechanism study's matched controls. Trigger: before the live view lists the climax as a pattern of its own.
- **How much of the flow-persistence events is "price recently rose or fell"?** Three or five minutes of one-sided flow fire on a large share of minutes and lean toward reversal; the cluster study puts most indicator events into two "price recently moved" clusters, and the overlap of the persistence events with them was not measured. Size: an hour (add the events to `cells_profile.event_masks` and rerun the overlap table). Trigger: before the persistence events are shown beside the cells in the live view.
- **The mechanism of the reversal, at the level of the book.** The tick-table study (docs/horizons.md, "The mechanism") excluded the bounce and left price impact unidentified. Settling it needs best bid and ask and depth snapshots around the same events (spread, depth and refill after a sharp move), which this repo does not have, and a pressure measure that sees depth. Size: unknown until a quote or book history is chosen and checked for coverage; the analysis itself reuses `mechanism.py`. Trigger: a quote or book history is available for the event dates, for instance the tracking layer's quote collection (see "Real quotes" above) once it has run long enough to hold events.
- **Longer histories for the other coins and venues.** ETH, SOL and DOGE klines back to listing, the futures series from 2019, Coinbase and Bitstamp histories. Size: a day of downloads and a multi-asset rerun, as one bounded replication matrix with frozen definitions, evaluated by period and leave-one-venue-out rather than pooled (correlated venues are robustness, not independent evidence). Trigger: the dashboard and viewing layer are built (owner, 2026-10-01: tabled, not dropped).
- **Real quotes for the cost question.** The 5-basis-point bounces cannot be traded on spot (horizons.md); whether a window contract carries the edge after its spread and fee needs quotes this repo does not have. Size: unknown. Decided 2026-10-01: the tracking layer (not the models) collects a binary-contract venue's quotes beside the forward log, so the forward period can measure information beyond the contract price (log loss and Brier against the quote, value at the displayed bid and ask, not the midpoint). Trigger: the tracking layer exists.

## Product

- **Link to the site.** Once calledit.money is deployed, the README and the forward-log section of the report link to it (the site is built in a separate private repo that reads this log). Size: ten minutes. Trigger: the deploy.
- **Live view on the tracking page.** Every minute: the active patterns, their next-5-minute probability and years held, the implied contract move, shown separately from the window call; reads the same tables and the forward log. Size: a week with the design frames. Trigger: the owner's design frames are ready (owner, 2026-10-01: fleshed out, more to add).

## Known limits

- XGBoost fits differ slightly between GPU and CPU, so a model's accuracy can differ by a few hundredths of a point between tables generated on different devices; the walk-forward table is the reference.
- September 2026 is a holdout, not forward: the log was first written after the month had passed. The forward log proper begins 2026-10-01.
- The first year of every nine-year study takes its volatility cutoffs from its own first quarter, because no earlier year exists; later years use the previous year.
- The tercile cutoffs in the volatility table come from the previous year, so a calm year after a wild one has most of its minutes in the low tercile. This is deliberate: the tercile must be known at the time.
- The forward log is computed on GitHub Actions, not on the owner's machine; the CSV in the repo is the serving copy until the per-window runner brings the database (the site repo's batch 2).
- Background runs are launched through `scripts/run_queue.py`, which waits for load; a job that seems not to start is usually waiting.
